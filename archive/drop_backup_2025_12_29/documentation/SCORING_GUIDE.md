# Scoring Guide & Philosophy

This document outlines the design principles and specific mechanics behind the scoring system in the "drop" Daily Domain Tracker.

## Core Philosophy

The scoring system is intentionally designed to move beyond simple gamification. It is not about achieving a "perfect 100" but about encouraging mindful engagement, rewarding consistency, and reflecting the qualitative nature of personal growth.

1.  **Realism Over Perfection:** Scores are intentionally capped and curved. The vast majority of scores for a good day will fall within a 75-85 range. Scores above this reflect exceptional, high-leverage effort. A perfect 100 is unattainable by design.
2.  **Qualitative Depth Over Binary Completion:** Simple binary toggles ("did it" vs. "didn't do it") are replaced with tiered levels that encourage self-assessment of the *quality* and *depth* of the practice.
3.  **Consistency is Key:** A 7-day weighted historical average is blended with the daily score. This means recent efforts have a greater impact, allowing for both the reward of building momentum and the realistic reflection of falling off track. You can't rely on a great start to the week to carry you through.
4.  **Mindful Engagement, Not Points Hacking:** Some activities, like meditation, are intentionally decoupled from the point system to preserve their intrinsic value and encourage practice for its own sake, not for a score.

## Domain Scoring Mechanics

### 1. Fitness Domain (Discipline & Consistency)
The goal is to reward a balanced fitness routine.
*   **Skill Practice (up to 40 pts):** This is now the highest-value component, emphasizing the importance of deliberate practice.
*   **Strength (up to 30 pts):** Replaces the binary toggle with three tiers of self-assessed effort.
    *   **Tier 1 (10 pts) - "Movement":** Light activity, like a few pushups at home, a walk, or a stretching routine.
    *   **Tier 2 (20 pts) - "Session":** A focused, shorter workout, like a brief gym session or a dedicated home workout.
    *   **Tier 3 (30 pts) - "Training":** A full, structured workout session.
*   **Running (up to 30 pts):** The scoring is now logarithmic to heavily reward the act of starting a run, with diminishing point returns for longer distances. This values the formation of the habit over raw endurance.

### 2. Mind Domain (Perception & Articulation)
The goal is to reward the depth and quality of intellectual engagement, not just the act itself.
*   **Reading (up to 50 pts):** Three tiers based on the quality of the material.
    *   **Tier 1 (25 pts) - "Leisure":** Casual reading, articles, or fiction.
    *   **Tier 2 (35 pts) - "Perspicacity":** Material that challenges your thinking and requires focus.
    *   **Tier 3 (50 pts) - "Erudition":** Deep, complex material that significantly expands your knowledge base (e.g., academic papers, dense non-fiction).
*   **Writing (up to 50 pts):** Three tiers based on the depth of articulation.
    *   **Tier 1 (25 pts) - "Journal":** Private reflection, note-taking.
    *   **Tier 2 (35 pts) - "Editorial":** Structuring thoughts for an audience (e.g., a blog post, a detailed email).
    *   **Tier 3 (50 pts) - "Treatise":** In-depth, structured writing on a complex topic.

### 3. Spirit Domain (Presence & Connection)
The goal is to encourage self-awareness without gamifying mindfulness.
*   **Mood Log (70 pts base + up to 30 pts bonus):**
    *   Simply logging your mood provides a base score of 70 points, rewarding the act of checking in with yourself.
    *   An additional 0-30 points are awarded based on your combined Mood (Valence) and Energy (Arousal) levels. Higher energy and a more positive mood contribute to a higher bonus.
*   **Meditation (0 pts):** Meditation is intentionally worth zero points. It is a practice meant to transcend the operational, goal-oriented mindset, and assigning a score to it would undermine its core purpose. It is tracked as a practice, but its value is intrinsic, not measured.

### 4. Sleep Domain (Recovery & Rhythm)
The scoring for sleep remains the same, as it is fundamentally a quantitative measure of duration. The goal is to get 7-9 hours of sleep for an optimal raw score.
